# Philippine building codes and design standards for commercial kitchens: verified research notes

Prepared 2026-11-23 for the Barro Kitchens (Barro Industries OPC) article series for architects and interior designers. Research only; no website edits. This is a citation aid, not legal advice. Local government units (LGUs), the Building Official, the BFP and the local health office interpret and enforce these rules, so every requirement below must be confirmed with them for a given project.

## 0. How to read this file

### 0.1 Confidence labels (used on every fact)

- **verified** = I read the instrument text myself from an official or officially hosted copy (DPWH, DOH, NCDA, lawphil, Supreme Court E-Library, FAOLEX copy of the DOH original), and where the copy was a scan I checked the key lines against the page image. Quotes are verbatim.
- **partly verified** = I read it, but only in a third-party transcription, an OCR text layer, or a low-resolution scan that I could not fully check; or the issuing instrument could not be identified exactly. Quote it only with the caveat shown, and re-check against the official copy.
- **inference** = my own reading of what verified text implies. Never publish an inference as if it were the law.

### 0.2 Quoting convention

- Philippine laws and regulations are quoted verbatim. Where the source is a scan, I corrected only obvious OCR character errors (for example "operale" read as "operate") and marked odd wording with [sic]. Always check the printed page before publishing a quote.
- Industry and foreign sources (copyrighted) are paraphrased, with a pin-cite.
- Pin-cites for the DPWH building-code PDF use PDF page numbers (the PDF is 432 pages), because its printed numbering is unreliable.

### 0.3 Access limits that affect confidence (important)

- The Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) website and all BFP regional download pages answered every automated request with a Cloudflare "Just a moment..." challenge (HTTP 403). I did not work around it (one attempt to read an archived copy elsewhere was refused by the environment's permission check, and I stopped there). **The official BFP PDF of the 2019 Revised IRR of RA 9514 could not be opened.** All 2019 RIRR facts below were read in a third-party transcription (VIZCODE, which names the 2019 RIRR PDF as its source) and are labelled "partly verified". One cross-check passed: the transcription contains the 2019 change to business occupant load (4.6 m² per person for concentrated use), which matches the change BFP itself reports.
- NFPA, ANSI/NSF and the Philippine Mechanical Engineering Code (PMEC) texts are paywalled or were not retrievable. Nothing numeric is claimed from them (see section 13).
- The DOE site for the LPG Industry Regulation Act IRR (JDC2022-05-0001) did not serve the PDF; only the statute was read.
- The Philippine FDA website pages returned 403 (CloudFront); two FDA PDFs were readable.

## 1. Summary

### 1.1 Solid enough to publish (with the pin-cites given)

- NBC / 2004 Revised IRR: occupancy groups (Group E Division 2 for dining establishments under 100 occupants), ceiling heights, kitchen minimum size (3.00 m², 1.50 m), window-opening ratio (10 %), artificial ventilation (Code 10 changes per hour for restaurant kitchens versus IRR Table VIII.4 figures, with the IRR's "more stringent prevails" note), chimney and fireplace-hood rules. (The 18.60 m² per occupant factor for commercial kitchens in IRR Table XII.1 is verified as a clause, but check the row alignment on the printed page first; see NBC-9.)
- RA 9514 statute: BFP fire safety inspection certificate is a prerequisite to the occupancy permit and the business permit.
- PD 856 and IRR Chapter III: sanitary permit, floors, walls, lighting (20 foot-candles = 215.2 lux), ventilation and exhaust rules, kitchen area (one quarter of dining area, not less than 9.30 m²), three-compartment sink, hot water at hand basins, grease trap, plan submission to the local health office, bakery rules.
- BP 344 statute and the restaurant and dining provisions of the 2024 revised IRR as hosted by NCDA (but see the instrument caveat in ACC-3).
- DOH AO 2014-0029 scope (restaurants and caterers are not FDA-licensed; processed-food manufacturers are).

### 1.2 Publish only with a caveat

- Everything from the 2019 Revised IRR of RA 9514 (hood suppression per NFPA 96 and NFPA 17A, extinguisher placement, LPG piping): third-party transcription only.
- Revised National Plumbing Code: grease trap and indirect waste provisions (scan; third-party host).
- DOH AO 153 s. 2004 design provisions (aggregator copy).
- Any statement that a kitchen "must" meet a ceiling height, an air-change rate or a classification threshold: these depend on how the Building Official reads the code (see NBC-3, NBC-7 and the open questions).

### 1.3 Do not publish

See section 13 (UNVERIFIED). Notably: Class K extinguisher requirement, PMEC hood and duct numbers, NFPA 96 numeric rules, NSF/ANSI 2 specifics, a legal "minimum aisle width" for kitchens, and any DOE rule on LPG cylinder storage at commercial premises.

### 1.4 The eight most useful facts for architects designing a commercial kitchen

1. **Ventilation figures conflict inside the NBC.** The Code says rooms "used as bakeries, hotel or restaurant kitchens" need "not less than ten changes of air per hour" (Sec. 811(b)(2)), while the IRR's Table VIII.4 gives "Restaurant Kitchen" only 5 air changes per hour at a 2.40 m ceiling (3 3/4 at 3.00 m; 3 at 3.70 m; 2 at 4.90 m; 1 1/2 at 6.10 m). The IRR itself says that on conflict "the more stringent restrictions must prevail". Design to the higher figure and have the Building Official confirm. (NBC-7)
2. **Occupancy thresholds differ between the Building Code and the Fire Code.** NBC: dining establishments "having an occupant load of less than one hundred persons" are Group E Division 2. Fire Code RIRR: restaurants and drinking establishments under 50 persons are mercantile; 50 or more are assembly. The classification drives everything else. (NBC-1, FC-3)
3. **Fire Code hood suppression.** Cooking equipment must be protected by an automatic kitchen hood fire suppression system per NFPA 96 and NFPA 17A, except small restaurants (under 50 occupants) using only outdoor, portable or warming equipment, and restaurants using small appliances with no hood or exhaust ducting. Cooking areas are treated as "high hazard" for extinguisher selection. (FC-4, FC-8; partly verified)
4. **Health-code geometry and finishes (IRR Chapter III of PD 856).** Kitchen area not less than one quarter of the dining area and not less than 9.30 m²; at least 215.2 lux (20 foot-candles) where food is prepared or utensils and hands are washed; floors graded and drained with 7.62 cm (3 in) coving; walls impervious to at least 2 m where wet or splashed; a three-compartment sink; hot and cold running water at hand basins (at least 37.8 °C); a grease trap placed outside where practicable. (SAN-8 kitchen area, SAN-4 lighting, SAN-2 floors, SAN-3 walls, SAN-14 sinks, SAN-9 hand basins, SAN-11 grease trap)
5. **Hood and exhaust details written into the health rules.** Hoods must prevent grease or condensate dripping onto food or food-preparation surfaces; grease filters must be commercial type and readily removable; discharge must not re-enter workplaces; recirculation limited to 75 % (workrooms) and 50 % (dining rooms); air velocity on workers not above 1.02 m/s. (SAN-6)
6. **Plumbing.** A grease trap is required "when, in the judgment of the Administrative Authority, waste pretreatment is required" for restaurants, cafés, bars, and hotel, hospital, factory or school kitchens; at most four fixtures per trap; food equipment must drain by indirect waste through an air gap of not less than 25 mm. (PLB-1 to PLB-4; partly verified)
7. **Accessibility (BP 344, 2024 revised IRR as hosted by NCDA).** 10 % of dining tables accessible; accessible table height 750 mm with knee space 700 mm high by 480 mm deep; two-level service counters (lower 750 mm, upper about 950 to 1100 mm); 900 mm clear route; 1500 mm circulation path in front of stalls. (ACC-3)
8. **Approvals chain.** Local health office approval of plans before construction, then a sanitary permit; BFP Fire Safety Inspection Certificate before the occupancy or business permit; an FDA License to Operate only for establishments that manufacture or distribute processed food, not for restaurants or caterers. (SAN-1, SAN-16, FC-1, FDA-1)

## 3. PD 1096 (National Building Code) and the 2004 Revised IRR

Sources (all read): [DPWH, IRR PDF](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf) (IRR plus the appended text of PD 1096); [Chanrobles, PD 1096](https://www.chanrobles.com/presidentialdecrees/presidentialdecreeno1096.html).

**NBC-1. Occupancy classification of restaurants, hotels, schools, hospitals.**
- Plain words: Dining and drinking establishments with an occupant load under 100 are Group E Division 2. Assembly buildings without a stage are Group H: Division 3 under 300 occupants, Division 2 at 300 or more. Hotels are Group B, schools Group C, hospitals Group D.
- Quote (IRR Rule VII, Sec. 701; same words in Code Sec. 701(a)(5)): "Division 2- Wholesale and retail stores, office buildings, drinking and dining establishments having an occupant load of less than one hundred persons, printing plants, police and fire stations, factories and workshops using not highly flammable or combustible materials and paint stores without bulk handlings."
- Quote (Group H): "Division 2- Any assembly building without stage and having an occupant load of 300 or more in the building." and "Division 3- Any assembly building without a stage and having an occupant load of less than 300 in the building."
- Quote (Group B): "Group B Occupancies shall be multiple dwelling units including boarding or lodging houses, hotels, apartment buildings, row houses, convents, monasteries and other similar building each of which accommodates more than 10 persons."
- Quote (Group D Division 2): "Nurseries for full-time care of children under kindergarten age, hospitals, sanitaria, nursing homes with non-ambulatory patients, and similar buildings each accommodating more than five persons."
- Inference: a dining establishment with 100 or more occupants is not named in Group E Division 2; the Building Official would place it by resemblance (NBC-2), most likely in a Group H division. Do not state this as the law.
- Cite: IRR Rule VII Sec. 701(1)(a); Code Sec. 701. [DPWH PDF pp. 54 to 56; Code text p. 369](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf)
- Confidence: verified (classification text); inference as marked.

**NBC-2. Unlisted occupancies go to the nearest group.**
- Quote (Code Sec. 701(b); IRR Sec. 701 repeats it): "Any other occupancy not mentioned specifically in this Section, or about which there is any question, shall be included in the Group which it most nearly resembles based on the existing or proposed life and fire hazard."
- Cite: Code Sec. 701(b). [DPWH PDF p. 56 and Code text p. 370](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf)
- Confidence: verified.

**NBC-3. Minimum ceiling heights.**
- Plain words: "Habitable rooms" with artificial ventilation: 2.40 m; in multi-storey buildings first storey 2.70 m, second 2.40 m; naturally ventilated: 2.70 m; mezzanines 1.80 m clear above and below.
- Quote (IRR Rule VIII, Sec. 805): "Habitable rooms provided with artificial ventilation shall have ceiling heights not less than 2.40 meters measured from the floor to the ceiling; provided that for buildings of more than one (1) storey, the minimum ceiling height of the first storey shall be 2.70 meters and that for the second story 2.40 meters and the succeeding stories shall have an unobstructed typical head-room clearance of not less than 2.10 meters above the finished floor. Above-stated rooms with natural ventilation shall have ceiling heights of not less than 2.70 meters." and "Mezzanine floors shall have a clear ceiling height not less than 1.80 meters above and below it."
- Caveat: the rule speaks of "habitable rooms". The IRR text I searched does not define "habitable room" (glossary pages searched), so whether a commercial kitchen is bound by Sec. 805 is a Building Official interpretation. See open questions.
- Cite: IRR Rule VIII Sec. 805; Code Sec. 805(a), (b). [DPWH PDF p. 135; Code p. 374](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf)
- Confidence: verified (text); applicability to commercial kitchens unverified.

**NBC-4. Kitchen minimum size (residential rule).**
- Plain words: A kitchen must be at least 3.00 m² with a least dimension of 1.50 m. (Bath and toilet 1.20 m², least dimension 900 mm; rooms for human habitation 6.00 m², least dimension 2.00 m.) The text does not distinguish residential from commercial kitchens, and I found no separate minimum for commercial kitchens in the NBC; no "dirty kitchen" provision exists in the NBC, IRR or Fire Code text I read.
- Quote (IRR Rule VIII, Sec. 806(1)(b)): "Kitchen – 3.00 sq. meters with a least dimension of 1.50 meters; and"
- Cite: IRR Rule VIII Sec. 806; Code Sec. 806(a)(2). [DPWH PDF p. 135; Code p. 375](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf)
- Confidence: verified.

**NBC-5. Natural light and ventilation: window opening ratio, air space.**
- Plain words: A room not provided with an artificial ventilation system needs windows with a total free opening of at least 10 % of the floor area (and not less than 1.00 m²), opening to a court, yard, public street or alley, or open watercourse. Toilets, bath, laundry and similar rooms: not less than 1/20 of floor area. Air space per person: 12.00 m³ in workshops, factories and offices; 14.00 m³ in habitable rooms.
- Quote (IRR Rule VIII, Sec. 808(1)): "Rooms intended for any use, not provided with artificial ventilation system, shall be provided with a window or windows with a total free area of openings equal to at least 10% of the floor area of the room, provided that such opening shall be not less than 1.00 sq. meter. However, toilet and bath rooms, laundry rooms and similar rooms shall be provided with window or windows with an area not less than 1/20 of the floor area of such rooms, provided that such opening shall not be less than 240 sq. millimeters. Such window or windows shall open directly to a court, yard, public street or alley, or open watercourse." (the "240 sq. millimeters" is printed that way; do not repeat it without checking)
- Quote (IRR Sec. 807(1)(b), (c)): "Workshop, Factories, and Offices – 12.00 cu. meters of air space per person; and" "Habitable Rooms – 14.00 cu. meters of air space per person."
- Cite: IRR Rule VIII Secs. 807, 808; Code Secs. 807, 808 (the Code text of 808 has only the 10 % rule). [DPWH PDF p. 135 to 136; Code p. 375](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf)
- Confidence: verified.

**NBC-6. Rooms housing heating equipment need artificial ventilation.**
- Quote (IRR Rule VIII, Sec. 811(1); Code Sec. 811(a)): "Rooms or spaces housing industrial or heating equipment shall be provided with artificial means of ventilation to prevent excessive accumulation of hot and/or polluted air."
- Cite: IRR Rule VIII Sec. 811(1). [DPWH PDF p. 136; Code p. 375](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf)
- Confidence: verified. (That a commercial kitchen is such a space is a reasonable reading, but it is mine.)

**NBC-7. Air changes for kitchens: Code and IRR disagree. This is the single most important accuracy trap.**
- Plain words (Code): restaurant dining rooms need not less than 3 air changes per hour; bakeries and hotel or restaurant kitchens need not less than 10 air changes per hour; both for rooms "entirely above grade". Plain words (IRR): "as shown in Table VIII.4", where "Restaurant Kitchen" is rated per person and per ceiling height, much lower than 10 at ordinary ceiling heights.
- Quote (Code Sec. 811(b)(1)): "For rooms entirely above grade and used for office, clerical, or administrative purposes, or as stores, sales rooms, restaurants, markets, factories, workshops, or machinery rooms, not less than three changes of air per hour shall be provided."
- Quote (Code Sec. 811(b)(2)): "For rooms entirely above grade and used as bakeries, hotel or restaurant kitchens, laundries other than accessory to dwellings, and boiler rooms – not less than ten changes of air per hour shall be provided."
- Quote (IRR Sec. 811(2)): "Whenever artificial ventilation is required, the equipment shall be designed to meet the following minimum requirements in air changes as shown in Table VIII.4. hereafter."
- IRR Table VIII.4 "Minimum Requirements for Air Changes", row "Restaurant Kitchen" (read from the page image): cubic meter per minute per person 0.34 (min.) to 0.43 (max.); air changes per hour by ceiling height: 2.40 m = 5; 3.00 m = 3 3/4; 3.70 m = 3; 4.90 m = 2; 6.10 m = 1 1/2. Other rows for comparison: Cafeteria 6, 4 1/2, 3 1/2, 2 1/2, 1 3/4; Cocktail Bar 8, 6, 4 1/2, 3, 2 1/4 (same ceiling heights).
- Quote (IRR Rule VIII, note 11 to the table of open-space requirements, Table VIII.3): "In case of conflict in the provisions on lighting and ventilation under this Rule or under the Code, the more stringent restrictions must prevail."
- Inference: at a 2.40 m ceiling the Code's 10 air changes per hour is the stricter figure; read together with note 11 it is the safer design basis. The Building Official decides.
- Cite: Code Sec. 811(b); IRR Rule VIII Sec. 811 and Table VIII.4; IRR Rule VIII note 11. [DPWH PDF pp. 131, 136, 137; Code p. 375 to 376](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf)
- Confidence: verified (all quotes; table read from the image). Chanrobles' copy of the Code has a typo in 811(b)(3); use the DPWH copy.

**NBC-8. Other rooms follow the PMEC.**
- Quote (Code Sec. 811(b)(5)): "For other rooms or spaces not specifically covered under this Section of the Code, applicable provisions of the Philippine Mechanical Engineering Code, shall be followed." IRR Sec. 811(3): "For other rooms or spaces not specifically covered under this Section, see applicable provisions of the pertinent referral code/s."
- Cite: Code Sec. 811(b)(5); IRR Rule VIII Sec. 811(3). [DPWH PDF pp. 137, 376](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf)
- Confidence: verified.

**NBC-9. Occupant-load factor for commercial kitchens, and exits.**
- Plain words: The IRR table of unit areas per occupant lists "Kitchens (commercial)" at 18.60 m² per occupant, with a second exit required where the number of occupants is over 50. "Drinking Establishments" are 1.40 m² per occupant, 30 occupants for the second-exit threshold. The Code itself leaves the factors to the Secretary; the table is in the IRR.
- Quote (IRR Rule XII, Sec. 1207(1)(a)): "The occupant load permitted in any building or portion thereof shall be determined by dividing the floor area assigned to that use by the unit area allowed per occupant as shown on Table XII.1. and as determined by the Secretary."
- Table XII.1 (continued; page image): rows "Stores-Retail Sales Rooms / Basement / Ground Floor / Upper Floors", "Offices", "Aircraft Hangars (no repair)", "Parking Garages", "Drinking Establishments", "Kitchens (commercial)" with unit areas 2.80, 2.80, 5.60, 9.30, 46.50, 18.60, 1.40, 18.60 and exit thresholds 50, 50, 10, 30, 10, 30, 30, 50. The labels and numbers are not aligned in the PDF; I matched them in order, which gives Kitchens (commercial) = 18.60 m² and 50. Confirm against the printed page before publishing a table.
- Cite: IRR Rule XII Sec. 1207 and Table XII.1. [DPWH PDF pp. 196, 197](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf)
- Confidence: verified for the clause; partly verified for the row-to-value alignment.

**NBC-10. Chimneys, flues, vents, hoods.**
- Plain words: The NBC regulates masonry chimneys, fireplaces and barbecues (Sec. 1211) and vent shafts and air ducts for light and air courts (Sec. 809). A keyword search of the IRR and Code text (hood, exhaust, duct, grease, cooking) found no separate rule for commercial kitchen exhaust hoods or grease ducts other than the ventilation provisions above and the PMEC cross-reference; about 90 of the 432 PDF pages have almost no text layer (figures and forms), and I did not inspect them one by one.
- Quote (IRR Rule XII, Sec. 1211, Hoods): "Metal hoods used as part of a fireplace or barbecue shall be not less than No. 18 gauge copper, galvanized iron, or other equivalent corrosion-resistant ferrous metal with all seams and connections of smokeproof unsoldered construction. The hoods shall be sloped at an angle of 45° or less from the vertical and shall extend horizontally at least 150 millimeters beyond the limits of the firebox. Metal hoods shall be kept a minimum of 400 millimeters from combustible materials."
- Quote (IRR Sec. 1211, Height, masonry chimneys): "Every masonry chimney shall extend at least 600 millimeters above the part of the roof through which it passes and at least 600 millimeters above the highest elevation of any part of a building within 3.00 meters to the chimney."
- Quote (IRR Sec. 1211, Cleanouts): "Cleanout openings shall be provided at the base of every masonry chimney."
- Caution: these are fireplace, barbecue and masonry-chimney rules. Do not present them as commercial kitchen hood rules.
- Cite: IRR Rule XII Sec. 1211; Code Sec. 1211(a), (b)(2). [DPWH PDF pp. 211, 212; Code p. 396](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf)
- Confidence: verified.

**NBC-11. Sanitation chapter cross-references.**
- Quote (IRR Rule IX, Sec. 903(3)): "Sanitary and industrial plumbing installations inside buildings and premises shall conform to the provisions of the Revised National Plumbing Code of the Philippines."
- Quote (IRR Rule IX, Sec. 905): "Garbage bins and receptacles shall be provided with ready means for cleaning and with positive protection against entry of pests and vermins." and "Dining rooms for public use without artificial ventilation shall be properly screened."
- Cite: IRR Rule IX Secs. 903, 905; Code Secs. 903(c), 905. [DPWH PDF pp. 166, 167](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf)
- Confidence: verified.

**NBC-12. Discrepancy to avoid copying: vent shaft area.** Code Sec. 809(a) says "not less than 0.10 square meter for every meter of height of shaft but in no case shall the area be less than 1.00 square meter"; the IRR Sec. 809(1) prints "not less than 1.00 sq. meter for every meter of height of shaft". Cite whichever you use, and flag the difference. [DPWH PDF pp. 136, 375](https://www.dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/IRR.pdf). Confidence: verified (both texts read).

## 4. RA 9514 (Fire Code of the Philippines) and the 2019 Revised IRR

Sources read: [RA 9514, lawphil](https://www.lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2008/ra_9514_2008.html) (statute: verified); [VIZCODE transcription of the 2019 RIRR](https://vizcodeph.com/code-library/ra-9514-fire-code-of-the-philippines/) (RIRR: partly verified). Official BFP PDF (NOT read, blocked): https://bfp.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/RA9514-RIRR-rev-2019.pdf

**FC-1. Fire safety inspection certificate gates the occupancy and business permits (statute).**
- Quote (RA 9514, Sec. 5(g)): "Designate a fire safety inspector through his/her duly authorized representative, who shall conduct an inspection of every building or structure within his area of responsibility at least once a year and every time the owner, administrator or occupant shall renew his/her business permit or permit to operate; No occupancy permit, business or permit to operate shall be issued without securing a Fire Safety Inspection Certification (FSIC) from the Chief, BFP, or his/her duly authorized representative;"
- Also Sec. 5(a): BFP shall "Issue implementing rules and regulations, and prescribe standards, schedules of fees/fire service charges and administrative penalties therefore as provided in the pertinent provisions of this Code".
- Cite: RA 9514 Sec. 5(a), (g). [lawphil](https://www.lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2008/ra_9514_2008.html)
- Confidence: verified.

**FC-2. The RIRR defers to other codes for gas and mechanical work.**

## 9. FDA and DOH rules for food manufacturers and commissaries

Sources read: [DOH AO 2014-0029, FAOLEX](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/phi174226.pdf); [FDA LTO checklist for food manufacturers, 2023](https://www.fda.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/1.-Initial-Food.pdf); [DOH AO 153 s. 2004 via Legaldex](https://legaldex.com/laws/revised-guidelines-on-current-good-manufacturing-practice-in-manufacturing-packing); [Supreme Court E-Library listing of AO 153](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/38817) (title and date only).

**FDA-1. Who needs an FDA License to Operate (LTO), and who does not.**
- Quote (AO 2014-0029, Part III, Scope): "This Administrative Order covers food establishments engaged in the manufacture and/or distribution, (i.e. import, export and/or wholesale) trade and/or repacking of processed food and food products." "It shall likewise not cover food businesses such as, but not limited to, activities in slaughter-houses, poultry dressing plants, fish ports, wet markets, supermarkets, school canteens, restaurants, catering establishments, water refilling stations, street food sale, including ambulant vending which are under the purview of the Local Government Units (LGUs)."
- Quote (Part V(A)(2)): "All food establishment shall secure a License to Operate (LTO) before engaging in food manufacturing, importation, exportation, sale, offer for sale, distribution, transfer and where applicable the use, testing, promotion, advertisement, and/or sponsorship of food products."
- Quote (Annex C, A.8): "Floor plan/ layout with dimensions" is among the LTO initial application requirements, with a location map.
- FDA 2023 checklist (food manufacturers, initial LTO): basic requirements "based on the Administrative Order No. 2020-0017" include an e-application, location plan and GPS coordinates, name of the qualified person and self-declaration; proof of business registration; proof of income or capitalization; and a "Site Master File" and "Risk Management Plan" to be presented to FDA inspectors during inspection; the page lists a pre-licensing inspection and a 20-working-day total. Fees are tiered by capital (for example Php 1,000 + 1 % LRF up to 250K).
- Inference: a commissary that only prepares meals for the same company's own restaurants is probably an LGU matter (restaurants and catering are excluded), while one that manufactures processed or packaged food for wholesale or sale to others is probably an FDA-licensed manufacturer. The AO does not say which applies to an in-house commissary. Confirm with FDA and the LGU health office.
- Confidence: verified (text); inference as marked.

**FDA-2. cGMP design points (for FDA-licensed manufacturers, including packers and repackers).**
- DOH AO 153 s. 2004 (7 May 2004), "Revised Guidelines on Current Good Manufacturing Practice in Manufacturing, Packing, Repacking, or Holding Food". Design and construction items (Part IV, Section B "Premises", item 2.3): "Adequate food safely [sic] controls through effective design and construction including the separation of operations in which food contamination is likely to occur, by one or more of the following means: locations, time, partition, air flow, enclosed systems, or other effective means." Floors "shall be of water-proof, non-absorbent, washable, and non-slip material, without crevices ... Floors shall slope sufficiently for liquids to drain to trapped outlets." Walls "water-proof, non-absorbent and washable materials sealed and free of insects and shall be light colored", with angles "sealed and covered". Drains "of adequate size with traps." "The processing of materials for food products shall be separated from the production of non-food products." "Toilets shall not be opened directly to production areas".
- Ventilation: "the direction of the air flow within the plant shall never be from a dirty area to a clean area."
- Equipment (Part IV, Section C, items 1 and 2): food contact surfaces "shall be corrosion resistant", "non-toxic", seams "smoothly bonded"; "All equipment shall be located and installed at least 1 meter apart." (This 1 m figure is a cGMP rule for plants covered by AO 153, not a rule for restaurants.)
- Cite: AO 153 s. 2004 Part IV, Section B (item 2.3) and Section C (items 1, 2). [Legaldex](https://legaldex.com/laws/revised-guidelines-on-current-good-manufacturing-practice-in-manufacturing-packing)
- Confidence: partly verified (aggregator copy; site says amendments not verified; E-Library confirms title and date).

## 13. UNVERIFIED — do not publish

Commonly repeated or plausible claims that I could not confirm in a source I read.

1. **"Commercial kitchens must have Class K portable extinguishers (Philippines)."** The Fire Code RIRR text I read lists Class A, B, C and D only and defines "Class K Fire" without requiring a class K extinguisher. Not confirmed; the RIRR does rate cooking areas as "high hazard" (FC-8).
2. **PMEC requirements for commercial kitchen exhaust**: capture velocity, airflow rate, duct material and gauge, cleanouts, fan type. The PMEC text was not retrievable.
3. **NFPA 96 and NFPA 17A numeric requirements** (clearance to combustibles, duct gauge, hood overlap, access doors, suppression nozzle coverage). Not read; the Fire Code only adopts the standards by reference.
4. **NSF/ANSI 2 scope and clauses.** Publisher and ANSI pages were not readable. Only the Food Code's acceptance of ANSI-accredited certification (4-205.10) is verified.
5. **A legal minimum aisle width or counter height for kitchens in the Philippines.** None found. The numbers in IND-4 and IND-5 are US or foreign planning guidance; the BP 344 heights apply to accessible tables and counters.
6. **A typical fixed work-surface height of 36 in or 900 mm.** No source read.
7. **That a commercial kitchen is a "habitable room" subject to NBC Sec. 805 ceiling heights.** Interpretive; the IRR does not define the term in the text I read.
8. **That restaurants with 100 or more occupants are Group H or any specific group.** Inference only (NBC-1).
9. **That the NBC 10 air changes per hour or the IRR Table VIII.4 figure is the one building officials apply.** Practice unknown (NBC-7).
10. **Grease interceptor sizing in the Philippines.** Sec. 1012 refers to Appendix B, which I did not read; any blog figure is unverified.
11. **BFP details from search summaries, not read:** RIRR approval on 7 Aug 2019 and publication on 16 Sep 2019; FSEC/FSIC fees; later amendments to the 2019 RIRR.
12. **DOE rules on LPG cylinder counts or storage at commercial premises.** The DOE-DTI IRR was not read.
13. **Philippine FDA AO 2020-0017 contents and the status of a 2024 draft AO that would repeal it; status of a draft "General Standard for Food Hygiene" to replace AO 153.** Secondary summaries only, not read.
14. **That the House approved the "New Philippine Building Act" in August 2023.** Reported by a search summary of a news article I could not open (403). Only the May 2026 Senate bill filing (Philstar.com) was read.
15. **Fixed claims about "Class A restaurant" placards as an architect obligation.** The classification belongs to the local health office (SAN-16).
16. **The metric unit for water pressure in IRR Ch. III Sec. 3(F)(3.2).** The scan prints "1.41 kpscm (20 psi.)"; cite "20 psi" only.
