Stainless Steel Work Table Guide: Sizes, Shelves, Grade and Finish

A work table looks simple until you have to choose one. This guide covers layers, shelves, size, grade, finish and what to check before you order.

In short

  • A work table, prep table and kitchen table are the same thing. Choose one to three layers and up to two upper shelves.
  • Measure the room, the doors and the aisles, then set the length, width and height. Choose a wall-type or centre table.
  • Ask for the grade and finish on the quotation. SS304 and hairline are standard; SS316 and other finishes are on request.

What a stainless steel work table is

A work table is a stainless steel table to work on. Work table, prep table, preparation table, working table and kitchen table all mean the same thing. It is a flat, washable surface for cutting, portioning and packing, with optional shelves above or below.

Stainless steel tables in the Philippines serve restaurant, hotel and school kitchens, bakeries, commissaries and laboratories. Ours are built to order, to your exact dimensions.

Layers and upper shelves

Each undershelf adds a layer to the top. Upper shelves sit above the top. Choose one to three layers and none, one or two upper shelves.

Layer options
LayersWhat you getThink about
OneThe top only.Open space underneath for bins, crates and a trolley.
TwoThe top and one undershelf.A work table with undershelf keeps pans and containers close, as along a cooking line.
ThreeThe top and two undershelves.More storage, such as trays in a bakery. The lowest shelf is harder to reach.

An upper shelf keeps condiments and small containers off the work surface. Skip it on a table two people work across, so they can see each other. For more room, add wall shelving above a wall-type table.

Length, width and height

You give us the length, width and height, so measure first. Our kitchen layout guide shows how to check aisles.

  • The room. Measure length, width and ceiling height at more than one point, because walls are rarely square. Note drains, water and gas points and outlets.
  • The way in. Measure the doors, corridors and stairs the table must pass through, at the narrowest point.
  • What stands on it. List the boards, containers and machines, and give the size and weight of any machine in your request.
Typical planning rules of thumb, not codes
MeasureRule of thumb
LengthThe wall run or floor space you have, less an aisle at each end.
Width (depth)Deep enough for your largest tray or board with room to work, and no deeper than you can reach across. A centre table worked from both sides can be wider.
HeightAbout 850 to 900 mm. Our dish tables, wok ranges, griddles, grillers and fryers use a standard 850 mm, so match a table beside one.
Aisle beside the tableAbout 1 metre for one cook, and about 1.2 to 1.5 metres where two people pass or a trolley rolls.

Wall-type table with backsplash, or centre table

Wall-type

Stands against a wall and is worked from the front. A backsplash, a raised back edge, closes the gap to the wall.

Centre or island

Stands in the open with an aisle on every side, so two people can work across it. With no wall behind it, it usually needs no backsplash.

In rows

Suits a school room or laboratory, as in the photo below. Plan the aisle between rows first, then fit the length.

A backsplash is not one of the listed options. Add it as a note on your request, say which sides touch a wall, and we will confirm it in the quotation.

Construction, grade and finish

Check these points in any stainless steel table supplier’s quotation. Ours are made like this:

  • Top plate. 1.5 mm (16 gauge). Undershelves and upper shelves are 1.2 mm.
  • Seams. Welded and ground smooth, so there is no joint for food and moisture to collect in.
  • Posts and feet. Square-tube posts with adjustable bullet feet, which help the table sit level, even on a floor that slopes to a drain.

Food-grade SS304 is our standard, and laboratory-grade SS316 is available on request. SS316 contains molybdenum, which resists salt and some cleaning chemicals better, so it suits laboratories, coastal sites and chemical exposure (Euro Inox, 2006). The two look the same, so ask for the grade on the quotation. See our SS304 vs SS316 guide.

Hairline is our standard finish, with a fine, uniform straight-line grain. Brushed, mirror or satin are on request. Brushed hides everyday scratches. Mirror shows fingerprints and fine scratches, so it suits a busy table less well (Euro Inox, 2011).

Care, and mistakes to avoid

Peel off the protective film, as in the photo at the top, once the table is installed and before it is used near heat. Wipe daily with mild detergent and a soft cloth along the grain, then rinse and dry (Euro Inox, 2011). Cut on a board, not on the steel. See our cleaning and care guide.

Avoid these three mistakes:

  • Measuring the room but not the door the table must pass through.
  • Choosing three layers when you need space for a bin or a trolley under the top.
  • Not saying which sides touch a wall, so there is no backsplash where you wanted one.

A checklist before you order

  • How many tables you need. Keep raw preparation on its own table, away from ready-to-eat food (U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2023).
  • Where each one stands, and which sides touch a wall.
  • Room measurements, and door and corridor widths at the narrowest point.
  • Length, width and height, matched to the equipment beside it.
  • Layers (one to three) and upper shelves (none, one or two).
  • Grade (SS304 or SS316) and finish (hairline, brushed, mirror or satin), written on the quotation.
  • Anything the options do not cover, such as a backsplash or the weight of a machine, as a note on your request.
  • A photo of the room and a rough sketch.

Frequently asked questions

How many layers and shelves should a stainless steel work table have?

Choose by what you want within reach. One layer leaves room for bins and trolleys, two add an undershelf for pans and containers, and three add a second undershelf. You can then add none, one or two upper shelves.

What size should a stainless steel work table be?

Measure your room, aisles and doors first. As a rule of thumb, a table is about 850 to 900 mm high, deep enough for your largest tray, with an aisle of about 1 metre beside it. These are planning guides, not codes. We build to the size you give us.

Can I order a wall-type work table with a backsplash?

A backsplash is not one of the listed options. Add it as a note on your request, tell us which sides touch a wall, and we will confirm it in the quotation.

Is SS304 good enough for a work table, or do I need SS316?

Food-grade SS304 is our standard and suits most kitchens. Laboratory-grade SS316 is available on request for laboratories, coastal sites and chemical exposure. The two look the same, so ask for the grade on your quotation.

Yes. We design, fabricate, deliver and install from Quezon City, across Metro Manila and Luzon. Request a quote online, or send a project brief for a larger job.
