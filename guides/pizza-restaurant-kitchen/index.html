Pizza Kitchen Equipment: Dough, Make Line, Oven and Storage

The oven gets the attention, but the work happens on the make line. Here is how to plan a pizza restaurant setup in the Philippines, from the dough to the cut-and-box counter.

A pizza kitchen is one short line

A pizza moves one way: dough, make line, oven, then the cut-and-box counter. Pizza kitchen equipment is mostly tables, trays and shelves around one hot spot, so draw the line first and choose the oven to fit it. Keep storage and washing to the side.

The pizza line, stage by stage
StageWhat to plan
Dough areaA mixer, a work table, dough trays on racks and proofing space.
Make lineA wide work surface with chilled toppings within reach.
OvenThe oven, its hood or flue, and its gas line.
Cut and boxA counter by the oven with shelves for boxes above.
Storage and washingRacks, shelves, chillers, and a sink with a drainboard.

Our kitchen layout guide shows how to check aisles on a scaled drawing, and our bakery equipment guide covers bread. This guide stays with pizza.

The dough area

Dough needs its own corner, away from the oven’s heat and the toppings.

  • Mixer. A general machine from a specialist supplier. Plan its footprint and power point before the tables are drawn.
  • Dividing table. Our work tables have one to three layers and up to two upper shelves, built to your size. The product page lists dough preparation tables with undershelves for trays.
  • Trays and racks. Stack dough trays on racks with three to six tiers, on wheels if a full rack must roll to the proofer. Tell us the tray size. See our trolley guide.
  • Proofing. Dough rises evenly in a warm, humid place away from draughts. The catalogue lists a 16-tray insulated dough proofing cabinet, 700 × 800 × 1850 mm, with a thermostat and humidity tray, for a 600x400 tray pitch, and a twin-door proofer-retarder with digital controls for retard then proof (read its catalogue note for what is included). Check that your trays match that pitch, and plan a power outlet beside it.

The make line

The make line is a wide, flat work surface where each base is stretched, sauced and topped. Plan it for speed:

  • Arrange toppings in the order they go on, sauce and cheese first, so the cook’s hand travels one way.
  • Keep chilled toppings in a rail of pans above a chiller, within arm’s reach.
  • Leave a clear stretch of table at least as wide as your largest pizza.
  • Put shelving above the line for dry toppings and spare pans, and a hand-wash sink at its head.

A pizza prep table is a chiller with a work top and a rail of pans. It is not in our catalogue, which lists refrigeration such as undercounter chillers and a freezer. We can fabricate stainless-steel items to your drawing (see fabrication), so send the chiller’s model and size, and the quotation will say what we can build around it.

Choosing the oven

The oven follows your style. Choose it early, because its size, heat and fuel decide the hood, the gas line and the room around it.

Deck-style oven

Pizzas bake on a deck and are turned by hand. It is not in our catalogue, so ask the supplier for its size, its decks and its power or gas needs.

Conveyor oven

Pizzas travel through on a belt, which suits steady volume. It is not in our catalogue, so ask the supplier for its length and its power or gas needs.

Wood-fired oven

It needs a flue and a place for wood and ash. It is not in our catalogue. See our pugon guide.

Ask the supplier for the oven’s temperature range and whether a baking stone fits before you choose it for pizza, and send us its outside size and fuel with your drawing.

A gas oven gives off heat and combustion gases, so plan a hood, exhaust and fresh-air supply with it (see our exhaust hood guide) and a sound LPG gas line. Ask the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) and the city or municipal building office what applies.

Cut, box and hand over

Put the cut-and-box counter beside the oven’s exit, so a baked pizza travels one step. A stainless-steel table with an upper shelf suits it: pizzas are cut on the table, and flat boxes wait on the shelf above, away from the oven’s heat and off the floor.

  • Leave one end of the counter clear for the handover to a rider or waiter.
  • Store spare boxes in a cabinet or on wall shelving nearby, so no one fetches them at peak time.

Storage, washing and heavy flour

A pizza kitchen holds more bulk than it looks: flour, sauce cans, cheese and boxes.

  • Flour sacks. They are heavy and attract pests. Keep them on the lowest tier of a rack, off the floor, and keep the open bag in a covered bin. Ask any fabricator, including us, what each tier is built to carry, because our racks page gives no figure.
  • Cans and dry goods. Wall shelving or a rack, with the oldest stock at the front.
  • Washing. Peels, trays and pans need a sink and a drainboard. See our sink guide.

Frequently asked questions

What pizza kitchen equipment do I need besides the oven?

A dough area with a work table, trays and proofing space, a make line with chilled toppings, a cut-and-box counter, racks and shelves, a sink and a hood. A mixer and a chiller are machines you buy separately.

What is a pizza prep table?

A work surface with a rail of chilled toppings above a chiller. It is not in our catalogue, so send the chiller’s model and size with a project brief, and we will say what we can fabricate around it.

Do I need a hood over a pizza oven?

Plan a hood or flue and a fresh-air supply with any oven, because gas gives off combustion gases and wood gives off smoke. Ask the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) and the city or municipal building office what applies to your unit.

Which pizza oven should I choose?

It depends on your style and volume. A deck-style oven suits a hand-made menu, a conveyor oven suits steady volume, and a wood-fired oven needs a flue. Ask your supplier for the temperature range before choosing one, and send us its size so we plan the table, hood and gas line around it.

Can you plan and install a pizza kitchen?

Yes. We design, fabricate, deliver and install stainless-steel work tables, racks, shelving, and sinks to your exact dimensions, with the hood and gas line planned around them. Send a project brief.
